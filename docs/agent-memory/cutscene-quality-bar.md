The first batch of Blender-authored cutscenes (intros, role scenes, gambit opener, meeting intro, commit bf4c46e) was called "horrible": the camera did not look at the characters, characters were badly positioned, and their animations were stiff and not expressive. The user asked for a full redo from scratch using the animation skill of the 3d-design plugin (atlas-agents marketplace, github.com/MonumentalSystems/Atlas-Agent-Teams; its `skills/animation/SKILL.md` is general principles: blocking → breakdowns → polish, anticipation, overlap, arcs, exaggeration, staging, review from several angles).

**Why:** hand-typed camera coordinates aimed at empty space; I accepted stills where actors were cropped, hidden or overlapping; poses were small and timid.

Round 2 (same day, on the rebuilt Diavolo/Muerto pilots): camera "miles better" but pacing awkward (too many short cuts, rushed beats), actors cramped and clipping walls/props/each other, set props clipping walls and floating (study window cut into the panelling; desk had its own lamp so added ones floated). Fixed with 4 longer cuts per 15 s, slow walks (walking(speed=)), and two checks that must read 0: `check_sets.py -- Sets` (props into walls/each other, floating) and `run_cinema.py -- Shot --clear` (every body part vs set and other actors; kneeling legs reach ~2 studs back, kneelers' arms need ~3.8 studs between centres beside a walker).

Round 3 (2026-10-11, watched in Studio): Blender previews lied. (1) Roblox's R6 avatars pivot on the Head, so PivotTo put heads on the marks and bodies 1.3-2.7 studs low; fixed by setting PrimaryPart = HumanoidRootPart (Stage.copyOf, CharacterService R6 rebuild, Bots). (2) Real avatar hair is ~2x a bare head, and the game letterboxes 11% top and bottom, so close-ups were all hair; previews now wear avatar-sized hair and the letterbox, and `--lint` prints `[frame]` warnings (camera within 3 studs of a head, head cut/low/filling the frame, face >60 degrees off camera or bowed). Debug panel Show tab has Shot x0.5/x0.25/x0.1 buttons: on-screen shot clock plus a `[ShotProbe]` print of where bodies really are at t=2. Ask before taking over the user's screen with computer use. Blender previews (`--sheet`/`--preview`) play the actions saved in art/blend/Animations.blend, not the shot module, so after changing acting run `run_anims.py -- --add <Clip>` BEFORE rendering (lint/--clear read the module directly). Muerto's cutscene = the anime's Mikami "delete" scene (user's reference, 2026-10-11), in the `flat` set; style target for acting = anime-fighter R6 timing (Jujutsu Shenanigans): holds, quick wind-ups, 2-frame strikes, overshoot+settle (`acting.strike`). Another agent owns gameplay emotes and the code-built rig (merged 2026-10-14).

Round 4 (2026-10-27): the user rates the other agent's emotes 10x better than my acting and asked me to build cutscenes their way. Their method (art/scripts/emotes/kit.py, mocap.py, art/refs/emotes/*.toml): a real reference video per clip, watched frame by frame, its beats (source time + pose) written down in a .toml; poses copied off those frames; hands put on targets with kit.reach (fist to CHEST, FACE, a pane...) instead of guessed angles; MediaPipe mocap for dances. I now write art/refs/cinema/<Shot>.toml first (IntroTokyo = Raye Penber's subway death). R6 lying down: only face down (root pitch -88) or on the back lies flat; on the side the body rests on the lower arm. Debug staging with `run_cinema --preview Shot --stills=t --eye=px,py,pz,tx,ty,tz,fov` before judging cameras. Inside a narrow doorway, put the second actor by its edge so a camera can see past them.

Round 5 (2026-10-27, Tokyo intro v2): "too fast, nothing understandable, station empty/unorganic, book from nowhere, he slides". Fixes that landed: 23 s in 7 held cuts (no cut under ~2 s); crowd spread across the platform with one walker passing through the action; props held in-hand before they drop; no root travel during staggers/falls. Cameras inside a train car looking out the doorway gave half-black frames (car wall) - shoot from the platform instead. A full-body R6 (5 studs) under the letterbox needs ~9.5 studs at fov 50. Probe part positions with a Blender-python script calling framing.part when a still renders black (camera inside a limb). Tokyo round 6: the Manual belongs to the watcher, never the victim (as in the source); a small prop action (shutting the book) needs its own insert close-up, high and frontal, or it doesn't read. The user likes dutch angles on the villain and motion blur (ShotPlayer BlurEffect + "fx blur" pulses); keep accessories 2+ studs from closing doors. Round 7 (2026-10-28, grilled): show cause→effect (pen's last stroke, hard cut on the jolt); the Cinema.impact streaks belong to the Muerto scene only (use pulse/drain/still/black events); platform shots get framing.pan(operator=True), the villain's stay locked; falls use a simulated topple and the free arm must lead the body down (else floor=True lifts the body); avatar hair hides bowed faces, so lift the head for the reaction shot. Previews: DG_EXPOSURE=4 for dark sets. Tokyo screen doors are static glass with a stripe/rail at head height. Round 8 (2026-10-28): the owner HATES heavy blur — the global Lens DOF was deleted ("DELETE THAT GLOBAL THING"); keep any focus very light. "Uninspired" means replace with something better, not remove: brainstorm and build (Tokyo got Cinema.ecg heart-monitor trace + a red/black still on the jolt). Collapses go lean → torso drops → legs give → fall forward, as one move. Next queued: the Diavolo + Muerto role rework (slashes along the pen strokes; Diavolo runs on through the role card).

Round 9 (2026-10-28): Diavolo + Muerto "still suck" after polish → redone from scratch (refs/cinema/RoleDiavolo.toml, RoleMuerto.toml; watched the anime via in-page canvas frame sheets on YouTube). Muerto's strokes must be SLASHES across the page (wind-up high, drag across in 2 frames, follow-through), not stabs. Lessons: place hands with cinema.acting.body_reach (kit.reach in a leaning body) and check the nib against the page numerically; keep heads UP in close shots (a bowed R6 head is only hair); follow-throughs go low/out, never across the face; set-desk props (screen, lamp) at head height block side angles — map them first and shoot front, low over the desk (page foreground, actor beyond) or high; the clearance check sees desks as top slab only, and big root turns swing knees into desks (cap ~11°, keep thighs level: hip = legs - lean). New fx "cctv" (Cinema.cctv). Animations.blend conflicts: take theirs, re-run run_anims --add per clip, stylua the Acting files.

Round 10 (2026-10-28, Campus + Agency intros redone): only the match's players in intros (twelve at most: "too many people otherwise"), no crowd extras; background people never share an animation in step (unique clips or offsets); falls and reveals get an anime speed ramp (fast, all but stopped, fast again); the Campus intro was "fine but a bit bland", so push mood and spectacle further next time. Big shared props (a solid map table, its chairs) decide where people can stand: list them with clearance.set_boxes first.

**How to apply:** aim every camera at the actor it films (compute look targets from the actor's actual head/body position at that time, not guessed coordinates); check every shot from its own camera AND from a wide/side debug view before showing it; block big, readable, exaggerated key poses with anticipation and follow-through; review stills of every cut, not one or two.
